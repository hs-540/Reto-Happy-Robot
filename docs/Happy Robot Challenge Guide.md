# Happy Robot Challenge Guide

Source: [HackSpain 2026 - HappyRobot Challenge](https://hackspain2026.happyrobot.ai/)

## Challenge title

**"Can AI manage a crisis?"** — HappyRobot's challenge for HackSpain 2026.

## Overview

Participants are invited to develop an artificial intelligence system that handles real-time emergency situations. Teams choose the type of crisis (fire, blackout, flood or another scenario) and must create an **autonomous agent** capable of making decisions while the environment continuously changes.

## Six key questions the system must answer

1. **Information relevance**: "A hundred messages come in and only three change anything."
2. **Prioritization**: determine which action is most urgent among multiple options.
3. **Selective communication**: "A neighbor, a firefighter and a manager do not need the same thing."
4. **Resource allocation**: distribute limited means among competing demands.
5. **Concrete action**: execute specific steps, not just describe the situation.
6. **Re-planning**: detect changes and adapt the strategy.

## Four agent competencies

- **Information gathering**: integrate data from multiple sources (calls, messages, sensors, APIs).
- **Prioritization**: "Working with the means that remain, not with the ones that would be needed."
- **Coordination**: execute real actions across multiple channels.
- **Adaptation**: modify plans in the face of situational changes.

## Submission requirements

| Requirement | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic system | Decides and acts on its own. A chatbot that only answers questions does not qualify. | Mandatory |
| Dynamic scenario | Continuous changes during execution. | Mandatory |
| Multi-step response | Chain of actions with a cohesive objective. | Mandatory |
| Real interaction | Integration with external systems (calls, APIs, tickets). | Mandatory |
| User interface | Panel to visualize state, system actions and intervene. | Mandatory |
| Learning | Analysis of previous runs for continuous improvement. | Bonus |

## Evaluation criteria

Three blocks of equal weight:

**Decision**
- Sound judgment with incomplete information.
- Identification of priorities under multiple urgencies.
- Adaptation to situational changes.

**Action**
- Simultaneous coordination of people, information and resources.
- Real execution versus mere proposals.

**Supervision**
- Operational transparency and human intervention.
- Originality of the scenario and its management.
- Capacity for iterative learning.

## Scenario examples

- Forest fire with dynamic re-planning.
- Widespread blackout with limited infrastructure.
- Armed conflict and civilian evacuation.
- Flood or natural disaster.
- Critical infrastructure failure.
- Original creative concepts.

## Provided resources

- HappyRobot platform (production with thousands of daily interactions).
- Technical support during the event.
- Emphasis on presentation quality: "The demo counts as much as the system."

## Notes

The page does not specify concrete event dates or schedule. It emphasizes that development must focus both on technique and on the persuasive presentation of results.
